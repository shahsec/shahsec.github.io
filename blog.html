5 Signs Your WordPress Site Is Hacked

November 2026

Many site owners find out about a hack weeks too late. Check your site for these warning signs:

  • Strange redirects — your site sends visitors to spam or gambling pages
  • Unknown admin users — accounts in wp-admin that you never created
  • Google warning — "This site may be hacked" in search results, or a red browser warning
  • Sudden slowdowns — the site becomes very slow for no clear reason
  • Weird content — new pages, posts or ads you never published

WordPress 500 Error or White Screen? 5 Common Causes

November 2026

Your site was fine yesterday — today it's a blank white page or a "500 Internal Server Error". These are the causes I see most often:

  • Plugin conflict — a plugin update clashes with another plugin or your theme
  • PHP version mismatch — your host upgraded PHP, but old code can't run on it
  • Memory limit reached — the site runs out of its allowed PHP memory
  • Corrupted .htaccess file — one bad rule can take the whole site down
  • Theme error — a broken edit in a theme file stops everything from loading

Randomly deleting plugins usually makes it worse. The safe way is to check the error log first, find the exact cause, then fix only that. If your site is down right now: see my WordPress bug & error fixing service on Fiverr.

Moving Your WordPress Site to a New Host? 5 Mistakes That Break Sites

November 2026

Migration looks simple — copy files, copy database, done. But these mistakes break live sites every day:

  • No full backup first — if anything goes wrong, there is no way back
  • Changing DNS too early — visitors land on a half-copied site
  • Forgetting SSL — the new host has no certificate, browsers show "Not secure"
  • Breaking email — domain email stops working when DNS records are missed
  • Not testing before going live — broken links, missing images and login problems found by your visitors, not you

Vulnerability Scan vs Penetration Test: What's the Difference?

November 2026

People use these two terms like they are the same thing. They are not:

  • Vulnerability scan — an automated tool checks your site against a list of known weaknesses. Fast and cheap, but it only reports what might be a problem — including false alarms.
  • Penetration test — a human tester actually tries to exploit the weaknesses, the way a real attacker would. You find out which flaws are genuinely dangerous and which are harmless.

A scan gives you a list. A penetration test gives you proof — plus a report that ranks every confirmed finding by risk and explains how to fix it. That is the service I provide: see my penetration testing service on Fiverr.
